Exploring Variation in Farming and Non-Farming Communities' Perception on Urban Amenities in Rural Areas of Bangladesh

Abstract

Before initiating any rural development programme to fulfil rural people's needs and expectations, it is essential to comprehend farming and non-farming communities' perception of urban amenities in rural areas. This study aimed to determine the socio-economic factors that influence farmers' and non-farmers' perceived need for urban amenities in rural Bangladesh. In addition, it explores how farming and non-farming communities' perception varies for different urban amenities. Data were collected through a questionnaire from a random sample of 452 respondents in rural areas of Dhaka, Mymensingh, Rajshahi and Chittagong divisions of Bangladesh. Descriptive statistics, inferential statistics and an ordered logistic regression model were used to analyse the data. Farmers' and non-farmers' perceptions were studied based on the quality and accessibility of urban amenities, their need for improvement, the extent to which they support people's livelihood, and their adaptation level. The results show that socio-economic characteristics, including gender, age, education level, monthly income and family type, have a significant influence on farmers' and non-farmers' perceived need for urban amenities in rural areas. Both groups perceived the provision of urban amenities in rural areas as important. However, their perceptions vary significantly for education, growth centres and local markets, economic services, community space, information and communication technology, and internet provision. The results provide practical guidelines for improving urban amenities in rural Bangladesh to support farming and non-farming communities.
