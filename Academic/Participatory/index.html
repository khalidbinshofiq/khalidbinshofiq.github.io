Objectives

  1. 01To understand the spatial and temporal extent of informal markets at the local level.
  2. 02To identify and analyse the problems faced by informal businessmen and users.
  3. 03To explore the relationship and role of stakeholders in informal markets at the local level.

Key findings

01Location
  • Advantages: steady demand from nearby households; little congestion or pedestrian conflict.
  • Disadvantages: space conflicts on the playground; weak visibility and access from New Polton Lane; limited reach beyond the neighbourhood.
02Infrastructure
  • Access to a WASA water pump.
  • Deficits in sanitation, drainage, storage and waste bins.
03Management & vendor rights
  • Operations depend on relationships with local leaders and the ward office; there is no formal committee.
  • No legal tenure, which exposes vendors to extortion and eviction, space disputes and restricted hours.
04Temporal dynamics
  • Seasonal: vegetables in winter and spring, fruits in summer, fish in the monsoon.
  • Weekly: peak on Fridays, with spikes during sports events on Fridays and Saturdays.
  • Festivals: more fish at Boishakh, less at Eid-ul-Azha; more fruit during Ramadan.
  • Daily: long commutes and gender roles shape routines; rain, waterlogging and heat disrupt storage and trading.
05Waste management
  • Open dumping, because there is no designated site, pollutes the market.
  • A local sweeper collects the waste and DSCC removes it; vendors pay a daily fee.
06Customer perceptions
  • Price rises are linked to extortion, maintenance costs and rainy-season scarcity.
  • Complaints about disruption of the playground, child safety, hygiene, and the privacy and security of nearby residents.
