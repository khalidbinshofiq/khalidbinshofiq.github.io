Planning context

The Planning Problem

Small towns combine fine-scale buildings, narrow corridors, vegetation, agriculture and small waterbodies. These heterogeneous landscapes are difficult to reconstruct with coarse imagery and can reward models that reproduce persistence rather than identify the relatively rare transitions that matter most for planning.

The study asks a practical question: can high-resolution historical imagery and progressively richer spatiotemporal models localize new urban growth in a data-scarce small town—and can those growth pressures be translated into evidence for evaluating a statutory master plan?

Data & methodology

Research Framework

Historical high-resolution RGB imagery was used to reconstruct LULC, spatial drivers were harmonized to a common 1 m analysis grid, and model comparison was performed using independent spatial test blocks before generating conditional 2031 scenarios.

  • Five LULC classes: built-up, waterbody, vegetation, agriculture and barren land.
  • Spatial drivers: DEM, distance to road and distance to built-up area (2011).
  • Drivers were reclassified to five ordinal classes and rescaled to 0–1.
  • Deep-learning input: two temporal slices, 128 × 128 patches, eight channels per time step.
  • Spatial leakage control: 1024 × 1024 blocks allocated to training, validation and independent testing before patch generation.
  • The same independent 2021 test blocks and target were used for all model comparisons.
Historical transformation

Two Decades of Urban Change

Kaliganj experienced substantial but spatially selective urbanization. The central nucleus expanded along major roads while agriculture remained the dominant land-cover class.

Common 2021 hindcast

Which Model Actually Captured Urban Growth?

Overall accuracy and planning-relevant change detection told different stories. Because most land remained unchanged, the persistence baseline achieved the highest OA while completely failing to locate new development.

Highest overall accuracy did not mean best urban-growth prediction. Persistence achieved OA = 0.69 but new-growth F1 and IoU were both 0.00. Evaluation therefore prioritized built-up and new-growth F1/IoU.

Responsible GeoAI

Where Is the Model Uncertain?

Predictive entropy was mapped to show where model probabilities were diffuse rather than concentrated. Higher uncertainty clustered around heterogeneous transition zones, central urbanized areas and development corridors.

Block-bootstrap confidence intervals overlapped substantially between the learned models. The U-Net–ConvLSTM therefore achieved the strongest change-sensitive point estimates, but the magnitude of its advantage varied geographically and should not be presented as statistically proven superiority.

From GeoAI to planning

Planning Implications

The value of the framework lies in turning a conditional model output into spatially interpretable evidence for plan review, infrastructure phasing and development control.

01

Development Control

The 8.45% of U-Net–ConvLSTM growth outside proposed areas highlights locations for closer monitoring, infrastructure review or future plan adjustment.

02

Phasing & Infrastructure

Proposal-only areas may represent longer-term capacity. Forecast pressure can help distinguish near-term growth fronts from land reserved for future phases.

03

Evidence-based Plan Review

GeoAI scenarios can interrogate planning assumptions and complement statutory planning without treating prediction as a substitute for normative planning decisions.

  • The historical archive contains only three dates and therefore only one temporal learning trajectory: 2001 → 2011 → 2021.
  • High-resolution RGB imagery provides fine spatial detail but less spectral information than multispectral data.
  • Spatial drivers were treated as static; built-up proximity was represented using the 2011 configuration.
  • Model skill is not uniform across all five LULC classes; planning interpretation therefore focuses on built-up and new-growth localization.
  • No post hoc manual correction was applied to the raw model outputs.
  • 2031 results are conditional scenarios and should not be presented as deterministic futures.
U-Net–ConvLSTM produced the strongest point estimates for built-up and new-growth localization under the common spatial hindcast. Its multi-scale encoder–decoder and skip connections are useful for fragmented built-up patches and irregular peri-urban boundaries. The improvement over ConvLSTM was modest, however, and overlapping block-bootstrap intervals mean the architecture should not be described as universally or statistically superior.

Research output: a planning-support framework, not just a prediction map

Fine-resolution historical reconstruction, spatially explicit GeoAI, uncertainty visualization and forecast–plan concordance are integrated to make land-change modelling more relevant to planning decisions in data-scarce small towns.
